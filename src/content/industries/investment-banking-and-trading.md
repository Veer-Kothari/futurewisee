## What Is Investment Banking and Trading?

Investment banking helps companies, governments and other organisations raise capital and complete major financial transactions. Depending on the firm and division, this may involve advising on mergers and acquisitions, issuing shares or bonds, and arranging financing.

Trading involves buying and selling financial instruments in markets. Trading activities may serve clients, manage risk, provide liquidity or implement investment strategies.

These are related areas of finance, but they are not the same job. Investment banking advisory work, sales and trading, research and asset management can have different responsibilities and career paths.

## What the Industry Does in Real Life

### Investment banking

- Advising companies on mergers and acquisitions.
- Preparing financial models and valuation analyses.
- Helping organisations raise equity or debt capital.
- Preparing presentations and transaction documents.
- Analysing companies, industries and financial performance.

### Trading and markets

- Executing trades in financial instruments.
- Analysing market prices, liquidity and risk.
- Responding to market news and client needs.
- Managing positions within authorised risk limits.
- Working with sales, research and risk teams.

## Main Divisions and Specialisations

- Mergers and Acquisitions (M&A)
- Equity Capital Markets
- Debt Capital Markets
- Leveraged Finance
- Sales and Trading
- Equity Research
- Fixed Income Research
- Structuring
- Quantitative Trading and Research
- Risk Management
- Private Capital Advisory, where offered

Investment banking, proprietary trading, brokerage, asset management and private equity are distinct activities, even where firms have overlapping businesses.

## Career Paths and Possible Jobs

### Early-career roles

- Investment Banking Analyst
- Sales and Trading Analyst
- Equity Research Analyst
- Quantitative Analyst
- Market Risk Analyst
- Trading Assistant or Junior Trader, depending on the firm

### Experienced roles

- Associate
- Vice President
- Director or Executive Director
- Managing Director
- Senior Trader
- Portfolio Manager, subject to the relevant career route

Titles and progression vary across firms and divisions.

## Skills Needed

### Technical skills

- Accounting fundamentals
- Financial statement analysis
- Valuation methods
- Financial modelling
- Excel and presentation software
- Understanding of markets and financial instruments
- Probability and statistics for relevant quantitative roles
- Programming for some quantitative roles

### Transferable skills

- Attention to detail
- Clear communication
- Working accurately under time pressure
- Teamwork
- Critical thinking
- Ethical judgement
- Understanding risk

## Qualifications and Industry Prerequisites

Possible academic backgrounds include:

- Finance
- Economics
- Accounting
- Mathematics
- Statistics
- Computer Science
- Engineering and other quantitative subjects

Requirements differ by role and employer. Some positions place greater emphasis on accounting and valuation, while quantitative trading and research roles may require advanced mathematics, statistics or programming.

Useful preparation:

- Learn financial statements.
- Practise basic company valuation.
- Build Excel and presentation skills.
- Follow economic and financial news.
- Explore legitimate internships and insight programmes.
- Understand the regulatory requirements for relevant market roles.

## Example Career Progression

Investment Banking: Analyst → Associate → Vice President → Director or Executive Director → Managing Director.

Markets: Analyst or Junior Role → Associate or Experienced Specialist → Senior Trader, Desk Head or Other Leadership Role.

These are common examples, not universal structures. Advancement depends on the firm, role and performance.

## Key Terminology

- **M&A:** Mergers and acquisitions, meaning transactions involving the combination or purchase of businesses.
- **IPO:** Initial public offering, a company's first public sale of shares under an applicable listing process.
- **Valuation:** Estimating the economic value of a company or asset.
- **Equity:** Ownership interest in a company.
- **Debt capital:** Funds raised through borrowing or debt securities.
- **Liquidity:** The ability to transact or convert an asset into cash without an excessive impact on its price.
- **Spread:** The difference between two prices or rates, such as a buying price and a selling price.
- **Volatility:** The extent to which a price or return fluctuates over time.
