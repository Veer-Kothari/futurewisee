## What Is Entrepreneurship?

Entrepreneurship involves identifying opportunities, developing products or services, building organisations and taking responsibility for the risks involved in creating or growing a business.

Entrepreneurs may launch new companies, develop technology products, build service businesses, create social enterprises or establish ventures within existing organisations.

Entrepreneurship is not simply having a business idea. It involves testing whether the idea solves a real problem and whether customers are willing to pay for the solution.

### What Does Entrepreneurship Look Like in Real Life?

Typical activities include:

- Researching customer needs.
- Testing products and services.
- Finding customers and building relationships.
- Managing cash flow and expenses.
- Hiring and leading employees.
- Raising finance, where appropriate.
- Adapting the business model when circumstances change.

## Career Paths and Possible Jobs

Entrepreneurship does not have one standard career ladder.

### Starting or running a business

- Founder
- Co-founder
- Small Business Owner
- Managing Director
- Chief Executive Officer

### Working in startups

- Business Development Associate
- Growth Associate
- Product Manager
- Operations Associate
- Marketing Specialist
- Customer Success Associate
- Chief of Staff, depending on the organisation

### Supporting entrepreneurs

- Venture Capital Analyst
- Startup Programme Manager
- Business Consultant
- Accelerator or Incubator Associate
- Innovation Manager
- Entrepreneurial Ecosystem Researcher

## Qualifications and Industry Prerequisites

There is no single degree required to become an entrepreneur. The appropriate background depends on the business.

Possible useful areas of study include:

- Business and Management
- Economics
- Finance and Accounting
- Computer Science and Engineering
- Marketing
- Design and Product Development

Other useful preparation includes:

- Building a small project.
- Interviewing potential customers.
- Learning to create a basic budget.
- Understanding contracts and business registration.
- Participating in permitted competitions or startup programmes.

## Example Career Progression

Possible route A: Identify a Problem → Validate Demand → Develop a Product or Service → Acquire Customers → Build a Sustainable Business → Scale or Diversify.

Possible route B: University or Early Career Role → Startup Employee → Team Lead → Operations or Product Leadership → Founder or Executive.

These are illustrative routes, not guaranteed outcomes.

## Key Terminology

- **Business model:** How a business creates, delivers and captures value.
- **Revenue:** Income generated from business activities before deducting costs.
- **Profit:** The amount remaining after relevant expenses are deducted from revenue.
- **Cash flow:** Money moving into and out of a business over time.
- **Product market fit:** Evidence that a product or service meets meaningful customer demand.
- **Bootstrapping:** Building a business using available resources and internally generated funds rather than relying primarily on outside investment.
- **Venture capital:** Investment in businesses with growth potential, often in exchange for equity.
