## What Is the Finance Industry?

Finance involves managing money, funding activities, evaluating financial performance, allocating capital and making decisions about financial resources.

Finance is broader than investment banking. It includes roles in businesses, banks, investment firms, insurance companies, government institutions and other organisations.

A finance professional might help a company decide whether to open a new facility, analyse its financial performance, manage its cash, evaluate an investment or assess financial risks.

Possible academic routes include:

- Finance
- Accounting
- Economics
- Business
- Mathematics
- Statistics
- Data Science

Some roles may value professional qualifications, depending on the country and career path. Others prioritise relevant experience, technical ability or licensing.

Useful student preparation:

- Learn to interpret financial statements.
- Build Excel skills.
- Understand revenue, costs, profit and cash flow.
- Practice presenting numerical findings clearly.
- Follow business and economic news.
- Explore appropriate work experience or internships.

## Example Career Progression

Financial Analyst → Senior Analyst → Finance Manager → Finance Director → Chief Financial Officer.

Alternative: Investment Analyst → Portfolio or Investment Manager → Senior Investment Leadership.

These are examples only. Career paths differ by employer and specialisation.

## Key Terminology

- **Revenue:** Income generated from business activities before relevant expenses.
- **Profit:** The amount remaining after relevant expenses are deducted from revenue.
- **Cash flow:** The movement of money into and out of an organisation.
- **Capital allocation:** Deciding how financial resources should be distributed among competing uses.
- **Forecasting:** Estimating future financial results using available information and assumptions.
- **Liquidity:** The ability to meet short term obligations or convert assets into cash.
- **Return on investment:** A measure comparing the benefit or return from an investment with its cost.
