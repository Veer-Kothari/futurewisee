## What Is the Law Industry?

The legal industry involves interpreting laws, advising clients, resolving disputes, drafting agreements and helping individuals, businesses and governments understand their legal rights and responsibilities.

Lawyers work in many settings, including private law firms, corporations, government departments, courts, international organisations and non-profit organisations.

### What Does the Industry Actually Do in Real Life?

Examples of legal work include:

- Reviewing contracts before a business transaction.
- Advising a company on regulatory requirements.
- Representing clients in disputes.
- Supporting mergers, acquisitions and property transactions.
- Helping organisations manage legal risks.

## Main Areas of Law

- Corporate and Commercial Law
- Criminal Law
- Civil Litigation and Dispute Resolution
- Employment Law
- Intellectual Property Law
- Tax Law
- Banking and Finance Law
- International and Arbitration Law
- Family Law
- Public and Administrative Law

## Career Paths and Possible Jobs

### Early-career roles

- Legal Intern
- Legal Assistant
- Paralegal, where applicable
- Trainee Lawyer or Trainee Solicitor, depending on the jurisdiction
- Junior Associate, subject to qualification requirements

### Experienced roles

- Associate
- Senior Associate
- Legal Counsel
- Barrister or Advocate, depending on the legal system
- Partner
- General Counsel
- Judge, subject to the relevant appointment requirements

### Alternative careers using legal knowledge

- Compliance
- Regulatory Affairs
- Contract Management
- Legal Operations
- Policy Research
- Corporate Governance

## Skills Needed

### Technical skills

- Legal research and interpretation
- Drafting and reviewing documents
- Understanding legislation and case law
- Constructing legal arguments
- Analysing evidence
- Understanding contracts and regulations

### Transferable skills

- Written and verbal communication
- Critical thinking
- Attention to detail
- Negotiation
- Organisation and time management
- Professional judgement and integrity

## Qualifications and Industry Prerequisites

- Relevant university degree or recognised legal qualification
- Professional training and examinations
- Admission, licensing or registration requirements
- Language requirements
- Internship and work experience opportunities

**Important:** Legal qualification is jurisdiction specific. Qualifying in one country does not automatically allow someone to practise in another.

## Example Career Progression

University or Legal Qualification → Professional Training → Junior Legal Role → Associate or Equivalent → Senior Role → Partner, General Counsel or Specialist Career.

## Key Terminology

- **Litigation:** Resolving a dispute through court proceedings.
- **Contract:** An agreement that may create legally enforceable obligations.
- **Due diligence:** Investigating relevant facts, risks and obligations before a decision or transaction.
- **Compliance:** Meeting applicable laws, regulations and internal requirements.
- **Jurisdiction:** The legal authority to hear a case or the territory whose laws apply.
- **Arbitration:** A dispute resolution process in which an appointed arbitrator or tribunal decides the dispute under the applicable rules.
