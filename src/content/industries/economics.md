## What Is Economics?

Economics studies how individuals, businesses and governments make decisions when resources are limited. It examines markets, incentives, production, consumption, employment, inflation, growth, trade and public policy.

Economics is not limited to working as an academic economist. Economic reasoning and data analysis are used in government, consulting, finance, research, business strategy and international organisations.

## What Economists Do in Real Life

Depending on the role, economists may:

- Analyse inflation, employment or economic growth.
- Research how a policy might affect households or businesses.
- Forecast economic indicators.
- Study consumer behaviour and market trends.
- Evaluate the costs and benefits of public projects.
- Analyse trade, investment or productivity.
- Prepare reports and briefings for decision makers.

## Qualifications and Industry Prerequisites

Common academic routes include:

- Economics
- Economics and Finance
- Mathematics and Economics
- Statistics
- Public Policy
- Related quantitative or social science degrees

Some research and senior economist positions may require postgraduate study. Entry level analyst roles may have different requirements.

Useful preparation:

- Learn to interpret economic data.
- Follow official economic releases.
- Practise writing concise explanations of economic issues.
- Build mathematical and statistical confidence.
- Explore data analysis and research projects.

## Example Career Progression

Research Assistant or Analyst → Economist or Consultant → Senior Economist or Manager → Research Director, Policy Leadership or Chief Economist.

Academic careers may require further research training and postgraduate qualifications.

## Key Terminology

- **GDP:** The value of final goods and services produced within an economy over a specified period.
- **Inflation:** A general increase in prices over time.
- **Opportunity cost:** The value of the next best alternative given up when making a decision.
- **Elasticity:** A measure of how responsive one variable is to a change in another.
- **Fiscal policy:** Government decisions about taxation, spending and borrowing.
- **Monetary policy:** Policies affecting monetary conditions and, depending on the framework, interest rates and liquidity.
- **Productivity:** A measure of output relative to inputs.
- **Econometrics:** The use of statistical methods to analyse economic data.
